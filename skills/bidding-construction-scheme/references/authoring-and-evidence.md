# Technical authoring and visual evidence

For the latest construction format, follow the 15-topic map in [latest-format.md](latest-format.md). Chapter 1 depth remains mandatory; the 200-page target is not a reason to omit BOQ methods. Remove duplicate generic text before considering any exception. Chapter 1 figures, the Word schedule figure (appendix 4), and the site-layout figure (appendix 5) serve distinct needs; do not substitute one generic workflow graphic for all three.

## Chapter 1 depth

Chapter 1 must be a project-specific method statement, not a generic catalogue. Normalize the BOQ into work packages and cover every material scope. For each method include:

1. scope and interfaces;
2. prerequisites, permits, surveys, isolation, and hold points;
3. crew, plant, tools, materials, and temporary works;
4. numbered execution sequence;
5. measurable quality controls and inspection records;
6. safety/environmental controls and stop-work conditions;
7. testing, commissioning, protection, handover, and as-built records;
8. explicit treatment of unknown design or owner-controlled values.

Prefer full sentences with technical cause-and-effect. Tie the method back to BOQ quantities/units and tender constraints. Cross-reference drawings and specifications by source location when available.

## Scope synthesis

- Group related BOQ rows into coherent work packages, but retain a row-to-package mapping so nothing disappears.
- Include demolition/protection, access, transport, lifting, waste handling, shutdown coordination, temporary services, testing, reinstatement, and handover where the evidence requires them.
- Explain interfaces between civil, structural, mechanical, piping, electrical, instrumentation, fire protection, and production operations.
- For hazardous media, define an owner-confirmed isolation/permit boundary, gas testing, ventilation, fire control, emergency withdrawal, and restart authorization. Do not invent concentration thresholds or valve lineups.

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the highest-authority available source:

1. user instruction and issued addendum/change;
2. owner-issued drawing/specification/task book;
3. BOQ descriptions and notes;
4. verified site information/photos;
5. current official standards;
6. conceptual diagram or professional inference, clearly identified.

When sources conflict, report the conflict and use the latest owner-authorized requirement. Do not silently average or blend values.

## Standards

- Verify standards live from official or authoritative sources when status matters; standards can be superseded.
- Cite only standards relevant to the actual scope and quote minimally.
- Record title, number/year, status, source URL/file, access date, and the clause/page used.
- Never reuse an older project’s standards list without checking applicability and current status.

## Figure plan

Use figures to explain a difficult relationship, sequence, interface, exclusion zone, or quality-control point. A typical complex Chapter 1 may use about six figures, adjusted to content:

- overall work-front and interface map;
- isolation/permit/energy-control flow;
- demolition and protection zoning;
- installation/testing sequence;
- quality-control/inspection hold-point flow;
- emergency response or commissioning logic.

Figure source preference:

1. cropped formal drawing or standard excerpt with page/figure citation;
2. deterministic diagram created from verified facts;
3. AI-generated conceptual illustration for non-dimensional spatial explanation.

When using image generation, request no embedded text, labels, dimensions, logos, or watermarks. Add editable captions and labels in the document. Inspect the image before use. Never let a generated figure override issued drawings.

## Prohibited fabrication

Do not invent equipment weights, centers of gravity, crane capacities, rigging ratings, lifting points, bearing pressures, test pressures, weld procedures, support spacing, hazardous-zone classes, PLC setpoints, cable sizes, material grades, or acceptance values. Convert missing data into a prerequisite, verification step, or hold point.
